Global value chains from the supplier perspective

I am a Senior Lecturer in Management at the University of Stirling. My research looks at global value chains from the perspective of upstream suppliers, particularly firms in developing economies. I am interested in what these firms can do when they have limited power and resources, and how their position shapes their ability to become more resilient, sustainable and competitive.

Research

Re-centering the supplier in global value chains

My research contributes to international business and global value chain scholarship by paying particular attention to suppliers, especially disadvantaged and peripheral suppliers in developing economies. A recurring question across my work is simple: what room do these firms have to act when they operate with fewer resources and less bargaining power than the organisations they supply?

I have explored that question from different angles over time. Some studies examine how suppliers build resilience. Others look at upgrading, sustainability, knowledge and technology, or the way governance affects supplier opportunities. The empirical settings have ranged from software and apparel to agro-food and aquaculture.

01

Power & Governance

Who has influence within a value chain, how that influence is exercised, and what it means for suppliers trying to improve their position.

02

Resilience

How suppliers cope when their operating environment becomes unstable, from political uncertainty to major shocks such as the COVID-19 pandemic.

03

Sustainability & Upgrading

How suppliers improve economically, socially or environmentally, and why the possibilities for doing so differ across local contexts.

04

Knowledge & Technology

How access to knowledge and new technologies can strengthen supplier capabilities, while also changing relationships within the value chain.

Teaching & Academic Leadership

Building around research and education

I am Programme Director of the MSc Business and Management and lead development of the new MSc Supply Chain and Logistics Management at the University of Stirling. A central part of my teaching is giving students opportunities to use academic ideas on real organisational problems.

I also founded and lead Interdisciplinary Research in Supply Chain and Logistics (IRiSCaL). It brings researchers from four University faculties together around shared questions in supply chains and logistics.
