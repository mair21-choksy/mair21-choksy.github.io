Research

Re-centering the supplier in global value chains

My research contributes to international business and global value chain scholarship by placing greater analytical attention on suppliers, particularly disadvantaged and peripheral suppliers in developing economies. The connecting question is how firms with constrained power and resources exercise agency within global production systems.

This programme has developed across resilience, governance and power, knowledge connectivity, economic and social upgrading, environmental upgrading, transparency and digital technologies. Empirically, it spans software, apparel, agro-food and aquaculture value chains.

01

Power & Governance

How governance structures, platform power and supplier positions shape opportunities for upgrading and value capture.

02

Resilience

How suppliers respond to political instability, institutional adversity, pandemics and other disruptions across global value chains.

03

Sustainability & Upgrading

How global mandates interact with local realities as suppliers pursue economic, social and environmental upgrading.

04

Knowledge & Technology

How knowledge connectivity, absorptive capacity, digital technologies, transparency and traceability affect supplier development.

Teaching & Academic Leadership

Building structures around the scholarship

I am Programme Director of the MSc Business and Management and lead development of the new MSc Supply Chain and Logistics Management at the University of Stirling. My teaching and programme work connect research-led education with authentic organisational problems, live-client assessment and employability.

I also founded and lead Interdisciplinary Research in Supply Chain and Logistics (IRiSCaL), bringing together researchers across four University faculties. The network has supported interdisciplinary publication, public scholarship, funding development and business engagement.
